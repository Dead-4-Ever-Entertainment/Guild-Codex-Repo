Atlas

Continents and biomes

The world is ten metal-named continents. Each holds ten towns and renames every landscape: the Copper Mainlands’ forest is the Ancient Forest, the Bronze Sanctuary’s is the Whispering Woods, and so on out to the Aetherium.

How a landscape is chosen

The town you just left carries a short list of biomes and a level band. The first wilds after that town takes the first biome on the list. Middle stages pick from the ones you have not used yet. The last stage of that hint is forced onto whatever biome is left, so you see the whole set before you move on. Twenty campaign steps is the old length of one biome when the road is marched in the fixed order: forest, grassland, coast, swamp, desert, mountain, tundra, volcanic, underground, Aetherium.

Travel you can actually take today runs the Copper Mainlands and finishes in the Bronze Sanctuary. The later continents are already named, leveled, and given portals; they open as campaign indexes climb. Every town on the atlas has a portal, and a portal opens with its town.

Grassland looks gentle and is not. Its written danger bonus is the highest of the ten: foes there are pushed eight levels. The Aetherium is next, at seven. Forest is the kindest, at zero.

The ten continents

Towns inside a continent step up by about five campaign indexes. The band on a town is the monster levels it considers fair. Stations differ: a castle on the edge of a continent tends to keep every bench, while a later village may have only a saw and an alchemy table. The full stall list is on the Towns page.
